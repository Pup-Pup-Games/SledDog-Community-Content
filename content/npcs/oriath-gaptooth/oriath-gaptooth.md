summary: She is the only copy of every debt in Gordin's Delve — and she's forgetting.
description: 'A small, round-shouldered woman of sixty-odd, in a shawl she has patched so many times none
  of the original wool is left. She sits where the light is, and she is always somewhere with a step.
  Her hands are ruined, knuckles swollen, fingers black to the second joint with twenty years of Foundry
  ink that never washed out, and she has not held a pen since she walked away from Jahalim''s counting
  house. She is warm, unhurried, and genuinely funny, and she will remember your mother''s name and what
  your mother owed. And under everything, always, just below speech: she hums. A low, tuneless, four-beat
  hum, over and over, even while she talks to you. It is not a habit. It is the sound of forty years of
  other people''s debts being kept from slipping.'
background: 'She was a clerk, and a good one, and for eleven years she wrote the books that turned wages
  into advances and advances into ownership, including, one bad winter, her own brother''s. He is still
  in the Delves. He will not speak to her. She walked out with nothing but what was in her head and has
  been giving it away one family at a time ever since, and because paper can be found and burned and tortured
  out of a man, she made herself the only archive: every debt the Slipped Tile has ever lifted, every
  name, every sum, held in a mnemonic she sings under her breath and has never written down. She is proud
  of that. She has not told anyone, not Bracc, not Little Ide, not a soul, that this past year the song
  has started coming up short. Two verses she is sure of. One she is not. Some nights she cannot find
  the winter of her brother''s name at all.'
drive: to un-write the whole Delve before the song goes. She is racing something and she will not say
  what.
fear: that the guild finds out she is failing, and starts keeping paper again.
secret: that she has kept copies of some ledgers instead of burning them, because a part of her still
  wants to be able to prove what was done. Including to her brother.

The best-informed person in Gordin's Delve keeps no books, because she is the books. Drop her in when your table needs a favor, a secret, or a debt erased — and let them slowly notice she's running out of time.
