# The Loom & the Dye-Pot
### A Stonetop Steading Improvement — looms, vats, and the colors that mark who you are

*A steading improvement for Stonetop. Drop it on the map between expeditions.*

> Anyone can keep warm in grey. It's the color that says we're somebody.

---

## What It Is
A weaving shed of clattering looms beside the homes, and a little apart, a dye-yard of steaming vats
stained blue and red to the rim, set near running water where the runoff won't foul the wells. Wool off
the flocks and flax off the Flats go in as thread; bolts of dyed cloth come out — festival dress, trade
goods, banners, and the deep woad-blue that has come to mean *Stonetop* to anyone who meets the village
on the road. The cloth keeps folk warm. The color tells them, and everyone else, who they are.

## Requires
*All of the following:*
- [ ] A site for the shed and looms, plus a dye-yard set a little apart by running water. Add it to the map.
- [ ] Pulling Together to raise the shed, build the looms, and sink the vats, requiring a season and
      timber and supplies (Value 2).
- [ ] A supply of fiber — wool from the flocks, flax from the garths retted in the stream.
- [ ] **Dyestuffs:** woad for blue, madder for red, weld and onion-skin for yellow, foraged from the
      Flats and the Great Wood's edge (which risks the Wood), or traded for rarer colors.
- [ ] A weaver-dyer who holds the craft, or a weaver and a guarded old dyewife, near full-time.

## When You Meet the Requirements
Increase **Fortunes** by 1, add "**the Loom & Dye-Pot** (dyed cloth, the steading's colors)" to the
**Resources** list, and draw it on the map. Give the dyer a name.

## Henceforth
```move
When the Seasons Change and you set the cloth on the looms and into the vats, roll +Fortunes:
10+ the bolts come off rich and true; gain Surplus, and you have color and cloth to spare.
7-9 the cloth comes, but choose 1 (or roll d4):
1. The dye-lot comes out wrong, muddy or blotched or faded. The bolt is near worthless and the dyer is furious.
2. Dyestuffs run short, and someone must forage deep at the Wood's edge for woad or madder (which risks the Wood).
3. The fine colors mark who has and who hasn't, and a quarrel over a festival bolt sets neighbor against neighbor.
4. A bolt comes off the loom bearing a pattern no one wove: a face, a road, a warning worked into the weave.
6- the GM says what spoils, runs short, or sows discord, or worsens the list above.
```

**Clothed in color.** When you Outfit or Trade & Barter fine or dyed cloth — festival dress, a trade
bolt, a banner, a gift meant to impress — you have what you need; Stonetop's colors precede it.

**Upkeep.** Looms and vats are only as busy as their supply. A poor shearing or a thin flax year, or
dyestuffs left ungathered, leaves the shed silent and the cloth grey. The dye-yard must also stay apart
and downstream, or its runoff fouls the water below.

## In Stonetop
- **The dyer** — stained to the elbows, recipes guarded jealously; quite possibly an outsider, or an
  old woman whose deepest blues come from somewhere in the Wood she will not name.
- **Color as belonging.** Stonetop's woad-blue becomes its mark, on banners, on the militia, on
  festival days. A thread straight into reputation, the Pavilion's festivals, and the small, sharp wars
  over who gets to wear the good cloth.
- **The Wood's colors.** The richest dyes grow at the treeline, and the deeper the color the village
  wants, the deeper someone has to go to fetch it. (Hooks into the Fae.)
