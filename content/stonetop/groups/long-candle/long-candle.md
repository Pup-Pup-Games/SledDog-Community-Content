summary: A heretical order in Marshedge studying the thing in the deep water.
description: 'Marshedge has no temple, so the Long Candle has no temple either — only a dry cellar beneath
  Edgemarket, dug down to the old Maker pavestone, because nothing unclean can set foot on a Makers''
  road. They are a dozen: a Lightbearer, two Blessed of the Great Mother, a Judge''s apprentice with her
  master''s habits and none of his authority, some herbalists, a fen-walker who ought to know better.
  What binds them is not a god. It is a book — one enormous chained ledger of every verified thing anyone
  has ever learned about the drowned thing the Marsh folk will not name, which they call, only among themselves,
  the Things Below. They keep a candle lit beside it, always, in turns, and their one iron custom is this:
  nothing goes in the book that a member did not see with their own eyes. No rumor. No scripture. Nothing
  they were told by something in the water.'
background: 'It started as four people and a disagreement. When the fen-walkers took a Mire family for
  congressing with vile spirits — the charter is clear, and the pyre was legal — a young Lightbearer asked
  the obvious question at the wrong volume: how does anyone know? No one could tell him what corruption
  was, only how to burn it. So he began writing down what was actually known, and found it came to less
  than a page. Forty years on it comes to four hundred, and every page of it is a crime. The Council would
  call it heresy; the fen-walkers would call it congress and be within their rights; Brennan would call
  it leverage, which is worse. So the Long Candle meets at night, on the stone, and every member has dictated
  their own last testament in advance, in case they do not come back from the water. What only three of
  them know is that the book has begun to include a section they did not choose to write — questions,
  in the margins, in a hand none of them recognize.'
agenda: to understand the Things Below. Not to worship them and not to fight them. They are convinced
  nothing bound can be unbound by people who refuse to look.
method: patient, verified, first-hand observation; bendis smoke, bronze, and the wayside stone; and the
  slow careful interviewing of anyone the water has touched.
faultLine: to study it you must get near it, and some of them have come back marked. Half the order says
  a marked member is their single most valuable source. Half says the charter is the charter, and holds
  that the moment they start making exceptions they have become the thing they are studying.

Everyone in Marshedge burns bendis root and looks away. These twelve burn it and take notes. A faith-order for tables running the Things Below — and a very good way to hand your players real, dangerous, verified information.
