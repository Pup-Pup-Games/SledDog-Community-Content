### A Stonetop Expedition — the first cut, and what it wakes at the Wood's edge

*Recommended: 3–5 players. A one-session first adventure. Target level 1.*

> It was the coldest winter anyone could name, and the woodpile was gone, and a desperate man
> walked to the edge of the Great Wood with an axe. He told himself one tree would not be
> missed. The Wood counts better than that.

---

## The Pitch (GM read-first)

Stonetop has one unbreakable law about the **Great Wood**: you may gather deadfall and fallen
limbs, but you **never cut a living tree.** It is the **Compact** — a promise the village has
kept for generations to the vanished Forest Folk, and the reason the Wood suffers Stonetop to
live in its shadow at all. Break it, the old folk warn, and you wake the Forest's wrath.

This has been the hardest winter in living memory. The woodpile is ash, and one cold snap is
still to come. **Hask**, a hard-luck farmer with a freezing family, walked to the Wood's edge
in the dark and felled a young, living birch — quietly, he thought, where no one would see.

The Wood saw. Now the cut stump weeps dark sap that will not freeze, the **boundary stone** is
split down its glyphs, and Hask has been found beside it: silent, frost-rimed, and **rooting
into the earth where he stands.** Something small and patient has crossed out of the trees to
collect what it is owed — *a tree for a tree* — and the green and the cold are creeping into
the village behind it.

**The main track:** go to the Wood's edge, learn what Hask did, and **pay the debt, close the
breach, or settle it by force and pay later** — before Hask takes root for good.

**Not a rail:** the split stone has laid bare a **Forest Folk hollow** to search; a small
living thing has crept into the impossible warmth for a kind PC to save; and a **wolf-pack**,
starved by the same cruel winter, is drawn to the blood and the warmth if the party dawdles.

**The driver is:** external (it sits at the Wood's edge) · nearby (a short walk from the
village green) · Stonetop's problem (Hask is one of theirs; the Compact is everyone's) ·
urgent (Hask roots deeper by the hour, and the cold is coming).

---

## Setup

After characters are made, ask these — to the *characters*, not the players. Spread them
around the table. **None of them gives away what has happened; they draw out who these people
are and what the Wood means to them.**

1. **(the hard winter — theme)** *This is the cruelest winter any of you can remember. When the
   woodpile ran low, what did your household burn last — and what did you swear you would never
   burn, no matter how cold it got?*

2. **(the Compact — lore, in their own words)** *Every child in Stonetop learns the one law of
   the Wood. Say it the way your elder said it to you — and tell us the story they used to
   frighten you into keeping it.*

3. **(fear of the tree line — character)** *You have felt watched at the edge of the trees. When
   was it? What did you do — leave an offering, say a word, run, or make yourself believe you
   felt nothing?*

4. **(a plain, dear bond — relationship)** *Name a neighbor whose hearth you would cross the
   frozen green to keep lit — someone ordinary and beloved, not kin to any hero. Why them?*

5. **(the Forest Folk — lore, optional)** *The Forest Folk are long gone, but their marks are
   still on the old boundary stones. What do you believe those marks are *for* — and who in
   Stonetop still keeps the old rites?*

> **Optional love letter** — to whichever PC named the dearest, plainest bond in question 4:
> *"For three nights now you have dreamed of the tree line — someone standing there in the snow
> with their arms out like branches, sunk to the ankle, and you could not see their face, only
> that they were being taken quietly and did not fight it. You woke each time with cold dirt
> under your fingernails. You have told no one. What do you fear the dream means?"*

## Get the Session Started

Read this aloud to open the table, then cut to Wren running across the green.

```read
It is the ragged, dying end of the hardest winter anyone in Stonetop can name. The woodpile is ash, too few chimneys are smoking, and the whole village lies hunched under old grey snow, leaning toward a spring that will not come. In the black hour before dawn, something is wrong. Frost-flowers are blooming on the inside of the shutters. A single green shoot has pushed up through the cold ash of a dead hearth. And every dog in the steading has gone silent at once and turned to point east, toward the black wall of the Great Wood. Then, out across the frozen green, a barefoot girl comes running through the snow — and she is screaming.
```

## Homefront

Open in the grey hour before dawn, deep in a dying winter. The village is hunched under old
snow; smoke from too few chimneys; the green is rutted ice. Frame a cold, close scene and make
homefront moves before you turn the knife:

- **Wren Greenless**, Hask's daughter, comes across the green at a dead run, barefoot in the
  snow, screaming for help — she went to bring her father in and found him at the tree line,
  *wrong*.
- **Old Yarrow**, the eldest who still keeps the rites, is already on her stick at the edge of
  the green, staring east at the Wood, lips moving in something that is half prayer and half
  curse. She will not go closer.
- Something is **off** in the village itself: frost-flowers blooming on the *inside* of the
  shutters; a green shoot pushing up through the ash of a cold hearth; the dogs gone silent
  and pointed east.

Let the PCs feel the cold and the wrongness. Then they see what Wren saw.

### The Hook — *"He won't come in"*

At the edge of the Great Wood, where a young birch was lately felled, the **boundary stone is
split** down the middle of its old carved glyphs, and the fresh **stump weeps a dark sap that
steams in the cold and will not freeze.** **Hask stands beside it,** stripped to his shirt
against all the cold, frost-rimed and silent, and his **bare feet have sunk into the frozen
earth** with pale roots curling out between his toes. He does not answer to his name. When the
wind moves the trees, he sways with them.

The hook is **actionable** (go to the Wood's edge, face what's owed), **involves everyone**
(Hask is theirs, the Compact is theirs, the village is watching from the green), and **urgent**
(he roots deeper by the hour, and the last cold of winter is coming in tonight).

> *Ask the table:* Which of you first understood that this was not sickness, but a *debt* — and
> what did the sight of Hask rooting cost you to look at?

---

## The Cast  *(who's in play)*

**Wren Greenless** *(keystone NPC — the daughter)*
Hask's daughter, old enough to know what her father did and young enough to believe the PCs
can undo it. Hollow-eyed, frost-burned, and certain it is her fault — *she* was the one who
cried with the cold; she thinks he cut the tree for her. Through tears and guilt she can tell
the PCs:

- Her father went out three nights ago with the axe and came back with wood and a look she
  didn't like. He told her, *"One tree. The Wood won't miss one tree."*
- Since then the frost won't leave him, and he's been walking to the tree line at night and
  standing there. This morning he didn't come back.
- She kept a **green-shooting splinter** from the wood he brought home — it won't stop growing,
  even cut from the tree. (Hand it to the PCs: proof, and a thread to the stump.)

She wants her father back, and to be told it isn't her fault. She'll give the PCs anything —
Hask's iron axe (the very one), her mother's rowan charm, her place by the fire.

**Old Yarrow** *(the rites-keeper)*
The eldest who still remembers the old words. She won't approach the breach, but she'll answer
what she's asked: the words of the Compact, the meaning of the boundary glyphs, and the cold
truth that **the Wood does not punish — it *collects.* A tree was taken. A tree is owed.** If
the PCs deal honorably, she is their surest ally after; if they answer the Wood with iron, she
is the first to name what that will cost.

**Hask** *(the debtor, half-claimed)*
Not a villain — a frightened man who chose his freezing daughter over an old promise. He can
still be reached in the early stages: he speaks slow, in the Wood's cadence, of *cold* and
*owing* and *Wren*. Later he speaks only in the voice of the trees.

**A neighbor who wants it burned** *(the wrong, tempting counsel)*
One villager at the green — a hard, frightened voice (name them at the table) — says the same
thing louder as the cold bites: *take the iron axe, cut Hask loose, and put the fell thing to
the torch before it spreads.* It would even *work.* It is exactly the counsel the Wood is
listening for.

---

## Heading Out

It is not a journey — it is a few hundred frozen yards from the green to the tree line, in
plain sight of the whole village. But the Wood's edge in deep winter is its own country. Let
them **Outfit** for the cold and decide together what they carry: Hask's iron axe (it will
work, and it will cost — see Dangers), Wren's rowan charm, a sapling or seed if anyone is
clever enough to bring one, a gift freely chosen.

**Winter-Wood impressions** — sprinkle these as they cross:

- Frost and a deathly stillness; the crunch and squeak of snow; breath hanging.
- Bare, boney trees reaching up into a white sky; no birdsong, none at all.
- The closer to the stump, the *warmer* — a wrong, green warmth, snow melted in a ring around
  the breach, and one impossible patch of living grass.

### Watch Out for…

- **The killing cold.** The last cold snap of winter rolls in as they work; lingering too long
  unsheltered is its own danger. The cold is the Wood's ally here — it presses them to hurry,
  and hurrying makes for rudeness.
- **A whiteout** off the trees if they anger the Wood early — a sudden snow-blind squall that
  separates the party and hides the tree line. Easy to lose your way a dozen steps from home.
- **The cold-starved** *(the fight you can reach for).* The same winter that broke Hask has
  emptied the Wood of easy prey. A gaunt **wolf-pack** has come to the tree line, drawn by the
  wrong warmth and the smell of sap and blood. They are not the Wood's servants — just hungry,
  and desperate, and unafraid of a village that has burned every fire down to ash. They can be
  driven off, fed, or fought; if the party bleeds at the breach or lingers, they close in.

### Risk Drawing Attention

The Wood is *listening* at a broken boundary. If the PCs are loud, bare their iron carelessly,
mock the old rites, take anything living from the trees, or refuse to treat with what comes,
the breach widens: **Hask roots a stage faster** (advance a grim portent), the wolves grow
bold, and the Hewn stops waiting and starts collecting.

> *Ask the table:* What small thing did each of you bring against the cold and the Wood — and
> which of you is quietly certain it won't be enough?

---

## The Wood's Edge  *(the site — a thin place, not a domain)*

Not a Fae realm — just the ragged seam where Stonetop ends and the Great Wood begins, made
thin and dangerous by the breaking of the Compact. A ring of wrong warmth in the dead of
winter, no wider than a barn, holding everything that matters.

### Impressions

- A ring of melted snow and impossibly green grass in the heart of winter; warm, and wrong.
- The cut stump, pale and wet, weeping dark sap that steams and will not freeze.
- The boundary stone split clean through its spiral glyphs — old Forest Folk work, broken.
- Hask, rooted and swaying, frost in his beard, eyes open and full of slow green light.
- The crowd of villagers gathered at a safe distance behind, watching to see what the PCs do.
- Small tracks in the warm mud that shouldn't be here in deep winter — something small and
  living crept in out of the cold.

### Features

**1 — The Boundary Stone** *(threshold, lore & offshoot)*
A waist-high standing stone carved with worn spiral **Forest Folk glyphs**, now split down the
middle. A careful reader (or Old Yarrow) can make out the old meaning: *the line you do not
cross, the tree you do not take, and what is owed if you do.* While the stone is broken, the
edge of the Wood is a thin place and the Compact does not hold. **Making it whole again**
(stone set right, the debt paid, the old words spoken) is one way to close the breach — and the
break has laid bare something no living hand has touched in an age (see **Side Opportunities**).

**2 — The Weeping Stump** *(the heart of it)*
Where the birch was felled. The cut is fresh and pale and *bleeds.* This is the wound in the
Compact made visible. Here the PCs can learn the truth (a tree was cut, a tree is owed),
examine the roots already reaching toward Hask, and — if anyone thinks to **plant a living
sapling in the breach and swear to tend it** — begin to make restitution. What is planted here
will not behave like an ordinary tree (see Threads Left Open).

**3 — Where the Hewn Comes** *(treat, or fight)*
From between the bare trees, walking, comes the thing the Wood has sent to collect. This is
where the PCs **treat with it** (its move, below) or take up iron against it. It is not hasty
and it is not cruel — it is *owed,* and it has all winter to wait. The villagers watching from
the green will remember what the PCs choose to do here.

> *Ask the table:* One of you has stood at this exact tree line before, on some ordinary errand,
> and felt nothing. What is different now that you can *feel* — and does it frighten you or draw
> you in?

## Side Opportunities  *(optional offshoots — reward the curious)*

- **The Split Stone's Heart** *(a Forest Folk find).* When the boundary stone broke, it opened
  a hollow at its core, packed with rotted wool and older cold. A PC who searches it — gently,
  and without bared iron — draws out a palm-sized **boundary-token**: pale heartwood and a
  thread of river-iron bound together, carved with a single spiral glyph, warm to the touch.
  Whoever carries it can *feel* where a line of the old Compact runs, and **once**, speak a word
  the Wood must pause to hear (hold the clock: freeze the Hewn, or steady a rooting soul, for a
  breath). Old Forest Folk work, freely given up by the breaking. *(A keeper's treasure — see
  Discoveries & Rewards.)*

- **Something living in the warmth** *(a companion for a kind hand).* In the ring of wrong-warm
  grass, a small creature has crept out of the killing winter to survive where nothing else
  could — a half-starved **fox kit** (or a snow hare, or a little owl; GM's pick, or ask the
  finder what they'd hope to find). It is *not* the Wood's making — just a cold, hungry thing
  that found the one warm place for miles, and it will bolt from noise and iron. A PC who is
  gentle, shares food, or tucks it warm may walk home with a **companion.** If the wolves come,
  it is also the first thing they'll hunt — saving it may mean standing between it and the pack.

> *Ask the table:* Whoever reaches for the small warm thing in the grass — when did you last
> have something soft and living to protect, and what happened to it?

## Dangers

**Hazards**
- **The killing cold** — the last cold snap of winter, pressing the PCs to hurry and punishing
  anyone who lingers exposed.
- **Rooting** — bare earth in the warm ring takes hold of what stands still in it. A PC who
  lingers barefoot, bleeds into the soil, or is gripped by the Hewn begins, slowly, to root.
- **Iron's double edge** — iron unmakes the Hewn and can cut Hask loose by force, but spilling
  iron at the broken boundary is a fresh insult to the Wood (see the Hewn's nature).

**The cold-starved** *(the smaller fight — always available, even if the party never draws iron
on the Hewn)*

```
THE COLD-STARVED — the hard winter's own hunger, come to the warmth
Group, small, hungry, wary
HP 6; ARMOR 1 (matted winter pelts)
DAMAGE bite d6 (close, pack)
SPECIAL QUALITIES: driven by starvation, not malice; scatter from fire and from a full belly;
  bold at the smell of sap and blood
INSTINCT: to survive the winter
 > Circle, test the party, and cut off the straggler
 > Lunge for the weakest, the warmest, or the small living thing in the grass
 > Break and flee when fed, burned, or badly hurt
```

**The Hewn** *(centerpiece — it would rather be paid than fight)*

```
THE HEWN — the tree that was cut, walking
Solitary, small, fae, magical, of-the-wood
HP 10; ARMOR 2 (greenwood), 0 vs. iron
DAMAGE splintering grasp d6 (close, grabby)
SPECIAL QUALITIES: Fae nature (p98); rooted to the breach (it cannot leave the cut grove);
  what it grips begins to take root; iron unmakes it but deepens the debt
INSTINCT: to balance what was taken
 > Show, without words, the stump and the man — what was taken, what is owed
 > Take root in someone who lingers, bleeds, or is held
 > Demand a tree for the tree, or a life to stand in its place
 > Turn the cold and the green against those who bare iron at the breach
```

> The Hewn is **not a monster to be slain** so much as a debt made to walk. It will not strike
> first; it waits, and it shows, and it collects. But it is of the Wood and **iron can unmake
> it** — and doing so at the broken boundary tells the whole Great Wood that Stonetop answers
> the Compact with steel. That bill comes due in spring.

**Impending doom:** Hask takes root entirely and becomes a young birch at the stump — a tree
returned for the tree taken, a life paid for a life. The Compact is *satisfied,* but at the
worst price, and the Wood remembers that Stonetop is the village that had to be made to pay.

**Grim portents** (advance with time spent, attention drawn, or the Hewn's moves):
1. *(hook)* Dawn: the split stone, the weeping stump, Hask rooted to the ankles and silent;
   green and frost creeping into the village.
2. Hask roots to the knee and speaks only in the slow voice of the Wood; the killing cold
   rolls in; the wolves gather at the tree line; the Hewn stands there, counting.
3. Hask roots to the waist; the village well rimes over with ice that will not thaw; the dogs
   flee; the Hewn comes into the ring to collect.
4. **Doom:** Hask becomes a birch sapling at the stump. A tree for a tree. The debt is paid in
   the cruelest coin, and the Wood turns its face from Stonetop come spring.

## Make Restitution to the Wood  *(custom move)*

> **When you offer the Wood amends** for the living tree that was cut, say plainly what you
> give. If you hold **leverage** — you offer a **living sapling** to plant in the breach and
> **swear to tend it**, you give something **freely and dear**, you **honor the Compact** out
> loud and set the boundary stone right, or you carry **no bared iron** — take **+1** and
> describe how. Roll **+WIS**.
>
> **On a 10+,** the Wood is satisfied; choose **2**.
> **On a 7–9,** it accepts, but choose **1** — the Hewn takes **1** more for itself, and the
> debt leaves a mark.
> **On a 6–,** it is not satisfied: the debt **deepens** (Hask roots a stage further) and the
> Hewn turns to collect it the hard way.
>
> - **Hask is freed** — the roots release him, fully, and he will mend.
> - **No new debt** falls on you, or yours, or the village.
> - **The breach is closed** — the boundary made whole, the Compact restored.
> - **You learn something true:** the old words of the Compact, or what the Wood remembers of
>   Stonetop.

**Ways the PCs might settle it** (reward any of them; don't telegraph one right answer):
- **Plant and swear** — set a living sapling in the breach and vow, by name, to tend it. A tree
  for a tree, freely given. The cleanest path — and it leaves something that must be kept (see
  below).
- **Give something dear, freely** — a heirloom, a year's labor pledged to the Wood, a true
  grief spoken aloud. Restitution need not be a tree if the giving is real.
- **Set the stone right and speak the Compact** — repair the boundary and renew the old promise
  out loud, with the village as witness (the boundary-token, if found, gives you the words).
- **Cut him loose with iron** — unmake the Hewn and chop Hask free. It *works,* and it is fast,
  and it tells the Great Wood exactly what Stonetop is. The debt doesn't vanish — it spreads to
  the whole village, and ripens in spring.

## Discoveries & Rewards

- **Learnable:** the truth of it — *a tree was cut; the Wood collects, it does not forgive; a
  tree is owed.* Hand the players the green-shooting splinter early and let them work it out.
  And the **words of the Compact** — what was actually promised to the Forest Folk, and what
  Stonetop owes for keeping its place in the Wood's shadow. Worth knowing; it comes up again.
- **Found / gained:** the **boundary-token** from the split stone's heart (a keeper's treasure,
  and the old words made portable — see Side Opportunities); a **small living companion** — the
  fox kit (or hare, or owl) from the warm ring, for the PC who was gentle with it; and the
  **green-shooting splinter**, a keepsake that will not stop growing.
- **On success / on failure:** a **clean restitution** earns the whole party Old Yarrow's
  trust — she'll teach one of them a rite of the boundary, and the village owes them a hard
  winter's gratitude. **Paying in iron** frees Hask but leaves a **mark**: the Wood learns that
  Stonetop answers the Compact with steel, and someone carries that enmity into spring.
- **GM's gifts** *(hand out at a good moment, if the table earns it):* **Hask's iron axe** —
  the very one, now heavy with what it did; and **Wren's mother's rowan charm**, freely given
  by a grateful girl.

## Aftermath / Threads Left Open

When it's done, however it's done:

- **Did they pay clean, pay dear, or pay in iron?** A clean restitution earns the village a
  hard, grateful winter's end and an elder's respect. Force frees Hask but stains Stonetop in
  the Wood's eyes. Either way, the village watched the PCs decide what kind of neighbors they
  are — that's a homefront thread for the whole year.
- **Hask and Wren** carry it forward: a man who was nearly a tree, a girl who blamed herself.
  Recurring faces.
- **Threads left open (→ E1 *First Thaw*):**
  - **The sapling in the breach.** What was planted at a broken boundary in deep winter does
    not sprout like an ordinary tree. Come the first thaw, it *wakes* — and what grows from a
    debt only partly understood is the Spring expedition's hook.
  - **A debt only half-settled.** Unless the PCs rolled a clean 10+ and paid in living green,
    *something* of the bargain lingers — a mark on Hask, a name the Wood learned, a promise to
    tend that must now be kept. The Wood wakes hungry in spring, and it remembers Stonetop.
  - **The thin place.** The Wood's edge here stays a little too warm, a little too watchful.
    The seam the cut opened does not fully close until spring — and maybe not even then.
  - **The boundary-token**, if it was found, is a thread of its own: the Forest Folk left it for
    a reason, and carrying their work marks you to whatever else still answers to it.
