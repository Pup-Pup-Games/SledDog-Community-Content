description: 'The Hwarrow mill sat where the millstream slows and spreads into the fen. A bad season and
  a worse flood drowned its lower floor a generation ago; the family that ran it left in the night and
  never said why, and the marsh has been taking the rest ever since. The roof sags green with moss, the
  sluice-gate hangs broken, and the great wheel turns slow and ceaseless in the current, grinding only
  silt and reeds. Folk on the Marshedge road give it a wide berth. Lately someone has not: there are fresh
  boot-prints on the one dry stair, and at night a shuttered light moves behind the upper shutters.'
impressions:
- The slow, wet knock-and-groan of the wheel, carrying across the fen before the mill comes in sight.
- Standing water gone black and still inside the lower floor, skinned with green; the smell of wet grain
  and rot.
- Floorboards that flex underfoot; a stair worn dry and clean by recent boots.
- Sacks of milled flour, ruined and burst, and one stack that is dry, roped, and waxed against the damp.
- Up in the rafters of the grain loft, something has made a nest of reed and bone, and it does not like
  the light.
